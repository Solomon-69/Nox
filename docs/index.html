Nox

One click flips your Mac between light and dark

Nox is a small menu bar app. Click its switch and your whole Mac goes dark or light. Add the Safari or Chrome extension and web pages go dark too, while Nox's own switch is on.

Mac: macOS 13 or later. Windows: Windows 10 or 11, with the free .NET 8 Desktop Runtime. Free, open source (MIT licence), no account needed.

What it does

Dark mode, one click away

macOS dark mode normally stops at the edge of the browser, so most websites stay bright. Nox fixes that while its switch is on.

One switch for the whole Mac

Click the switch in the menu bar to flip light and dark. Right-click it to open the panel.

Websites follow along

With the Safari or Chrome extension on, bright pages get a dark look while Nox is dark. Pages that already have a dark design are left alone.

Private by design

No account, no network access and no analytics. The extension only checks whether Nox's switch is on. It never reads or sends what you browse.

New in V2

A smoother switch, and a better Windows setup

V2 makes the change from light to dark gentler and easier to live with.

A smooth fade

Nox fades to dark or light and waits until every app has finished changing, so you never see a half-switched screen.

Other apps' menus stay open

Flipping the switch no longer closes menus that other apps have open.

Websites dark on Windows

There is now a short setup for websites on Windows too, so pages can follow the switch there as well.

Share with a friend

On a Mac, one click packs the app up and opens the share sheet, so you can send it to a friend by AirDrop.

Menus stay open

Click another icon in the menu bar and this panel stays open instead of closing on you.

No zoom on Windows

Pinch, Ctrl and the mouse wheel, and Ctrl with plus or minus no longer zoom the Nox panel.

Install

A few steps, then it stays out of the way

Nox is not sold through an app store, so your computer may ask you to confirm it the first time you open it.

MacYour computer

macOS 13 or later

  1. Download Nox.dmg, open it and drag Nox into Applications.
  2. Open Nox. A small switch appears in the menu bar.
  3. Follow the welcome window and press Allow on Apple's prompts. Nox needs this to change light and dark.
  4. For Safari: go to Safari, Settings, Extensions, tick Nox, then click Edit Websites and set it to Allow.
  5. For Chrome: in the Nox panel click Open extension folder. In Chrome, open chrome://extensions, turn on Developer mode, click Load unpacked and choose that folder.

Why the extra steps: browsers need permission before an extension can change how a page looks.

WindowsYour computer

Windows 10 or 11

  1. Download Nox-windows.zip and choose Extract All.
  2. If asked, install the free .NET 8 Desktop Runtime from Microsoft.
  3. Run Nox from the extracted folder. Click the icon in the system tray (near the clock) to flip Windows between light and dark.
  4. If Windows shows a warning, click More info, then Run anyway.

Why the warning: the Windows app is not code signed yet.

Questions

Good to know

Why do websites stay bright?

Check that Nox's switch is on, that the extension is ticked in Safari with Edit Websites set to Allow, and that the extension is loaded in Chrome.

Do pages change when my Mac goes dark by itself?

No. Web pages only go dark when Nox's own switch is on. Your Mac's dark mode on its own does not change them.

Why does it ask for Automation and Screen Recording?

Automation lets Nox switch light and dark. Screen Recording is only for the smooth fade: Nox takes one still picture at the moment of switching. The picture stays in memory and is never saved or sent.

Some sites look wrong when forced dark.

Forcing a dark look on every page cannot be perfect. If one looks odd, turn the website option off in the panel, and please report the address on GitHub.

Does it collect any data?

No. Nox, the Safari extension and the Chrome extension make no network requests and have no analytics or accounts. The full detail is in the privacy notes.

Unsigned app: how to open it

This app is not signed yet (Apple's developer account is paid, and one is not set up yet), so your computer warns you the first time. Mac: right-click the app, choose Open, then Open again (or System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway). Windows: in SmartScreen click More info, then Run anyway. You can check your download against the SHA-256 fingerprints listed in the project's CHECKSUMS.md on GitHub.
